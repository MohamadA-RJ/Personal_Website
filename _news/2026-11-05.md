## Published in Composites Science and Technology

Excited to share that our paper has been published in **[Composites Science and Technology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compscitech.2026.111912)**!

How can we learn the statistics of as-manufactured fiber misalignments from experimental data and calibrate a stochastic generative model that produces diverse microstructures while preserving these statistical characteristics, with customizable fiber volume fractions, fiber counts, size distributions and inter-fiber distances ?

To answer this, we developed a framework that converts X-ray µCT fiber paths into statistically equivalent, overlap-free 3D microstructures. It combines copula-based statistical modelling, autoregressive depth continuity, unique identification for high-misalignment fibers and Bayesian optimization with a strict but customizable physical overlap-free generation algorithm. 

---
## About the Work

Fiber misalignment governs the microscale failure of composites, yet experimentally resolved three-dimensional microstructures remain scarce, costly, and specimen-specific, limiting high-fidelity virtual testing. This work presents an **end-to-end framework** that turns X-ray μCT fiber paths into statistically equivalent, physically admissible 3D microstructures.

A developed **stochastic model** reproduces the measured descriptors by combining slice-wise empirical marginals, Gaussian-copula in-plane dependence, latent autoregressive depth memory, gated joint-tail enrichment, and a library of rare high-misalignment "motifs," all calibrated through Bayesian optimization. Driven by the calibrated model, a **slice-by-slice growth engine** with variable-radius seeding, Delaunay-based neighborhoods, and an ellipse-aware contact solver generates about **2,400 fibers**, while a closed-form between-slice capsule check certifies non-overlap over the full depth.

The verified microstructures are exported as non-intersecting solids importable into finite-element software, opening a scalable route to virtual testing, uncertainty quantification, and data-driven composite design.
