Fiber misalignment governs the microscale failure of composites, yet experimentally resolved three-dimensional microstructures remain scarce, costly, and specimen-specific, limiting high-fidelity virtual testing. This study presents an end-to-end framework that turns X-ray-μCT fiber paths into statistically equivalent, physically admissible 3D microstructures. A developed stochastic model reproduces descriptors by combining slice-wise empirical marginals, Gaussian-copula in-plane dependence, latent autoregressive depth memory, gated joint-tail enrichment, and a library of rare high-misalignment "motifs," calibrated by Bayesian optimization. Driven online by the calibrated model, a slice-by-slice growth engine with variable-radius seeding, Delaunay-based neighborhoods, and an ellipse-aware contact solver generates about 2400 fibers, while a closed-form between-slice capsule check certifies non-overlap over the full depth. The verified microstructures are exported as non-intersecting solids importable into finite-element software, opening a scalable route to virtual testing, uncertainty quantification, and data-driven composite design.
